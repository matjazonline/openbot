53: ## Department dispatch and escalation
54: 
55: Departments ([departments](departments.md)) organize desks into a hierarchy with a lead, and a
56: lead's job is to hand work to its members' desks and to escalate to its parent department's desk.
57: What holds today:
58: 
59: - **The lead knows where to send.** An `agent.run` on a desk its department owns gets the
60:   department's name, charter, whether the agent is the lead, and — on a desk that is not public —
61:   the addresses of the department's own desk, its parent's, its direct children's and its agents'
62:   own desks, frozen with the run (coordination C003).
63: - **Relays are admitted.** A relay into a members-only desk is trusted like any other relay inside
64:   the company; members-only restricts people and outside senders (coordination C001). For people,
65:   a members-only desk admits its department's members and role holders, the parent department's
66:   lead and each direct child department's lead: one step up and one step down (coordination C002).
67: - **A send does not start the member's work.** A `message.send` to a member's desk is an outbound
68:   note on that desk; none of its bindings run. A member's reply travelling back along the trace to
69:   the lead's desk is refused as a cycle (see "Loops").
70: 
71: So dispatch that starts the member's run, and escalation that returns an answer to the lead, wait
72: for `channel.call` in agent-workflow-call, which the plan names as their successor (coordination
73: C004). Until then a lead can tell a person where work belongs, and people can forward it.
